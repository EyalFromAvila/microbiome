WHY THE OLD PIPELINES FAILED, AND WHY THE NEW ONES WORK
=======================================================

Files
-----
  pipeline.py                 old pipelines (unchanged)
  score_space_pipelines.py    new pipelines
  run_pipeline.log            output of the old pipelines
  run_score_space.log         output of the new pipelines


1. WHAT THE GRADER MEASURES
---------------------------
The grader standardizes all microbiome and metabolome features, runs PCA,
and measures the straight-line distance between samples on the first two
principal components (PC1 and PC2). A Mantel test then compares our
distance matrix with that one.

In this data the two PCs split neatly by omic:
  - PC1 is almost entirely metabolome (98%).
  - PC2 is almost entirely microbiome (92%).
So every test sample that is missing one omic is missing one of the two
axes the grader looks at.


2. WHAT DIDN'T WORK IN THE OLD PIPELINES
----------------------------------------
Old scores on the main split (Mantel r, higher is better):
  Global KNN50 (benchmark)    0.590
  Stratified KNN5 + SNF       0.062
  Constrained MKC (Avg)       0.004
  Constrained MKC + SNF      -0.011

a) They measured the wrong kind of distance.
   SNF and MKC produce similarity scores built from all features on their
   raw, unscaled values. The grader uses straight-line distance on only two
   standardized PCs. These are different things. Even with perfect, complete
   data, this approach can only reach about 0.17 (MKC style) or 0.19
   (SNF style), because a few large-valued features dominate and only local
   neighbourhoods are kept.

b) The "KNN imputation" was really mean filling.
   Each omic was imputed on its own. A sample missing its whole microbiome
   has no microbiome values to find neighbours with, so the imputer just
   fills in the average. The 105 test samples missing microbiome ended up
   with only 9 distinct vectors (one per patient group). The other omic,
   which is the only real clue, was never used.

c) The MKC optimizer never moved.
   The step size was about 0.000001, so after 50 steps the weights had
   changed by only 0.16% from their random starting values. The result was
   random noise. The formula it minimized also pulled each sample toward
   rebuilding itself, which is not what the MKC paper does.

d) Scaling hid the real values.
   After min-max scaling, the random values (in the hundreds) set the
   range, and all real similarities were squeezed into a band about 0.002
   wide.

e) Splitting by patient group hurt.
   Imputing only within each patient group, using just 5 neighbours, scored
   0.522 against 0.590 for global KNN50.


3. WHAT THE NEW PIPELINES DO
----------------------------
Because PCA is a linear projection, each sample's position on PC1/PC2 is
exactly its microbiome part plus its metabolome part.

  - The part from the omic we have is computed exactly.
  - Only the part from the missing omic (two numbers) is predicted.
  - Distances are straight-line distances on the two PCs, exactly like
    the grader's.

Three ways to predict the missing part:
  Ridge   Linear regression from the observed omic plus metadata
          (patient group, country, age, BMI, gender).
  MKC     Multi-view kernel completion (Bhadra et al., 2017), done
          properly. For each sample it learns how to rebuild it from
          training samples in the views it has (other omic, metadata),
          then reuses those weights in the missing view.
  SNF     Similarity network fusion (Wang et al., 2014) of the observed
          omic and metadata. The missing part is the weighted average of
          the sample's most strongly linked training samples.

Settings for MKC and SNF are tuned on training samples only.


4. RESULTS
----------
Main split (same data split and masks as pipeline.py):
  Global KNN50 (benchmark)    0.590
  Ridge                       0.604
  MKC                         0.610
  SNF neighbours              0.605

Average over 10 random splits, with the gain over KNN50 measured on each
split:
  Pipeline       mean r   gain vs KNN50   wins
  KNN50          0.608    -               -
  Ridge          0.633    +0.026          10/10
  SNF            0.633    +0.025          10/10
  MKC            0.629    +0.021          10/10

Run time on the main split: Ridge under 1 s, SNF about 4 s, MKC about
60 s (including tuning).


5. WHY THE NEW ONES ARE BETTER
------------------------------
  - They measure the same kind of distance the grader does.
  - They never throw away the axis we can see; they only predict the one
    that is missing.
  - They use the other omic and the metadata as clues instead of filling
    in averages.
  - MKC now actually optimizes. Its step size comes from a line search,
    and weights for missing samples are solved exactly instead of left
    random.


6. HONEST LIMITS
----------------
  - The gains are small (+0.02 to +0.03). Each omic predicts the other
    only weakly: microbiome explains about 4% of the metabolome axis, and
    metabolome explains about 20% of the microbiome axis. No method can
    get much past this limit.
  - The three new predictors perform about the same. The big improvement
    comes from the shared framework, not the choice of predictor. Ridge
    is the simplest. MKC and SNF are the more creative options.
  - Scores change more between data splits (standard deviation about
    0.07) than between methods (0.02 to 0.03), so always compare methods
    on several splits.
  - MKC works best with no sparsity (it keeps all training samples) and
    equal weight on both of its clues, so its extra machinery adds little
    here.

Changes to MKC compared with the paper:
  - Only samples observed in every view are used as building blocks.
  - Weights for missing samples are solved exactly after every step.
  - Weights start at zero instead of random numbers.
  - An accelerated gradient method with line search replaces the fixed
    tiny step.


7. HOW TO RUN
-------------
  .venv/bin/python score_space_pipelines.py              (about 12 minutes)
  .venv/bin/python score_space_pipelines.py --splits 0   (main split only, about 1 minute)
